Story:

In the 1760s, Joseph Black observed that equal masses of different substances (like water and mercury) mixed at different temperatures did not reach the mathematical average temperature. He discovered that all materials have a specific type of range to hold heat.

Citation : Black, Joseph. "Lectures on the Elements of Chemistry." Classic Chemistry, edited by Carmen Giunta, Le Moyne College, web.lemoyne.edu/giunta/blackheat.html. Accessed 23 Apr. 2026.

For a long time, people thought that if a material was heavy and dense, it would hold heat longer. However, this has been suggested to be inaccurate by Benjamin Thompson and James Prescott Joule.

Therefore, we have decided to test it out ourselves. Our group has decided to investigate which substance (paper, plastic, porcelain, glass, or stainless steel) has the best heat resistance.

Our purpose of this project is to learn more about thermal conduction, and determine which cup will provide the best heat insulation for hot liquids, as we can use the information in real life. We are going to pour water that is 60˚C into all of the cups, and measure how long it takes to cool down to 50˚C.

Background Info:

Thermal insulation is the reduction of heat transfer from one object to another. Heat transfer occurs through three methods: conduction (heat flowing through a material), convection (transfer in liquids and gases), and radiation (energy traveling through a medium or space). Thermal conduction refers to how easily heat moves through a specific material.

Material Properties:

  • Porcelain: High heat resistance; lightweight, good insulator, poor conductor.
  • Stainless Steel (Double Wall): High heat resistance; corrosion and temperature resistance.
  • Glass: Medium-high heat resistance; low thermal shock resistance, poor thermal insulator.
  • Paper: Medium heat resistance; tearing resistance, poor conductor, good insulator.
  • Plastic: Low heat resistance; cannot withstand high temperatures. Plastic cups lose heat the fastest, making them less ideal for those who prefer their drinks warm.
Order of Best Thermal Insulators:
Porcelain > Stainless Steel > Glass > Plastic > Paper

Heat Capacity:
The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of an object by 1°C.
Formula: Q = mcΔT (Heat = Mass × Specific Heat × Change in Temperature)
